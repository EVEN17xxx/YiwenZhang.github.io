Yiwen Zhang

PhD Researcher, Department of Psychology, University of Limerick

Department of Psychology

University of Limerick, Ireland

I’m a PhD researcher in the Department of Psychology, University of Limerick, supervised by Mike Quayle. I study how networks of attitudes and meanings become aligned with social identities, so that a small set of expressed positions can reveal broader patterns of group membership.

My work looks at this question from three angles: who stays silent in attitude surveys and why, agent-based simulations of social pressure and disengagement, and how speakers are positioned in news discourse. Across all three, I treat what goes unsaid — nonresponse, silence, omission — as part of the social information system, not as missing data to be deleted or imputed.

Before my PhD, I completed an MSc in Computational Social Science at Linköping University and a BSc in Social Work at Nanjing Institute of Technology. I work mainly in R and Python, and draw on network analysis, survey methodology, and agent-based modeling.

Beyond research

Outside the lab, I’ve served on the board of the LinCSS Association at Linköping University, organizing welcome events and alumni engagement for Computational Social Science students, and volunteered at Vattentornet Student Pub, co-hosting multicultural events like the Chinese Spring Festival celebration.
